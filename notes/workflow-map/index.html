BUSINESS OPERATIONS / PRINTABLE TEMPLATE · 13 SEPTEMBER 2026

Find the wait before
automating the task.

Map how work moves from request to accepted result. The best opportunity may be in the hand-off rather than the drafting step.

A hypothetical example

A weekly report takes one hour to draft, two days to collect missing inputs and another day to obtain approval. These are example timings, not measured results.

Ask where the work stops.

Faster drafting would help with one part of that example. It would not resolve missing information or an unclear approval owner. Map the full process before choosing an AI tool.

Your one-page workflow map

Print this worksheet and use one real process. Record observations before estimating savings.

1. Outcome & definition of done

What must the next person be able to do? Who accepts the result?

2. Trigger & required inputs

What starts the work? What information is required, and who supplies it?

3. Steps, owners & hand-offs

Name the owner of each step. Identify where responsibility changes.

4. Active time & waiting time

Measure them separately using the same start and end points.

5. Exceptions & decisions

What happens when information is missing or the output cannot be accepted?

6. Smallest useful improvement

What could you change, who owns it, and how will you know it helped?

Choose the intervention after the map.

A repeated transformation may suit automation. Missing inputs may need a clearer request. An approval queue may need a named decision-maker. The map makes these possibilities visible without assuming that every delay needs AI.

After a change, compare elapsed time and accepted outcomes across similar work. Record changes in workload or complexity so you do not credit the tool for a difference it did not cause.
